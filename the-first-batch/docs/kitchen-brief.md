# Creative Brief · The Kitchen · Dystopian American Dream

Album cover concept for **The First Batch**, the debut LP by **Chillsbury Doughboys** (CBDB). Written Nov 11 2026 from Josh Titus's freewrite (verbatim at the end) and three explore rounds that missed. Josh is the creative director. Hand this whole file to a new session.

## The project in one paragraph

Chillsbury Doughboys is a five piece instrumental band from Denver (rock, dance, funk, jazz, jam). Fans wear chef hats and aprons to shows. Puns stay subtle, never slapstick. Every image is generated with Gemini image models (Nano Banana). There is no photoshoot. The cover is a 1:1 square that has to read at thumbnail size on Spotify and may run with no type at all. A locked cover already exists, a record pressed out of raw dough. This kitchen is an alternative concept that is still being found.

## The idea in one line

**A memory caught in the act of lying.** A lower middle class American kitchen in 1995, shown as the warm, perfect picture we carry in our heads today, with the real one leaking through.

## The tension the image has to hold

- **Then vs the memory of then.** The American dream as it was lived in 1995 against the version grown adults replay in 2026.
- **Today vs the pining for then.** "It was all so much easier back then" is a hiding place. The image shows the hiding place starting to fail.
- **Every revisit inflates the facade an inch.** The memory is not a record. It's a set we keep redecorating, with plastic fruit and faux sunlight that were never there.
- **The hologram is dimming.** The protective glow flickers and the buried struggle shows through: the money that never came, the renovation that never happened, the hard nights. The image doesn't take a side. It sits in the flicker.
- **Questions, not answers.** The viewer should feel something happened and not be able to name it. Strange, offbeat details invite a second look.

## The scene

- **When.** 1995. Shot from the point of view of a grown child looking back from 2026.
- **Who.** A family of four, lower middle class. Not rich, not poor. The common experience. No people appear in frame, only their traces.
- **Where.** A suburban starter home they moved into in 1995. The kitchen is a leftover from the 1970s because there was never money to renovate. That means 70s orange tile (Josh's call, no wallpaper), teak or walnut slab cabinets, an avocado fridge and range, a wood topped breakfast bar peninsula with chrome stools in orange vinyl. Twenty years of wear: lifted linoleum edges, a cabinet door that never closes, a water stain on the ceiling.
- **1995 layered on top.** A cordless phone or a beige wall phone with a long coiled cord, a TV with a VCR on the counter, fridge magnets and kids' drawings, a Tupperware tower, a calendar, mail and bills, a boom box, a cereal box, juice pouches, a box of macaroni. Plastics in the era's colors: teal, grape, magenta, lime, butter yellow. These happen to be the album's nine track colors (Blueberry, Butter, Cherry, Bondi, Bubblegum, Lime, Grape, Tangerine, Mint), so the 90s plastics can carry the brand quietly.

## Memory vs truth · how each shows up

| | The memory (the facade) | The truth (what's buried) |
|---|---|---|
| Light | Golden afternoon sun that never sets, soft glow, a warm haze on everything | Cold flickering fluorescent tube, green cast, hard shadows |
| Surfaces | Glossy, plastic, waxed, too clean, shrink wrapped | Worn tile, grout gone grey, chipped laminate, exposed 2x4 studs behind drywall |
| Window | A perfect sunny yard with a white picket fence, painted on like a backdrop | Grey, a chain link fence, a parked car, nothing |
| Objects | Plastic fruit, a ceramic rooster, a full cookie jar, matching dish towels | Past due bills in pink and yellow, a phone off the hook, moving boxes never unpacked |
| Baking | The first batch of cookies, golden and perfect on the tray | The same tray burnt, a smoke detector hanging open with its battery pulled |
| Feeling | Safe, warm, simple | Tired, anxious, making do |

## How the image phases between the two · directions to explore

Each is a different mechanism for "the hologram beginning to dim." Explore several. They can combine.

1. **The light line.** One frame split by light. The memory half sits in golden glow and the truth half in fluorescent green. The line cuts straight through objects, so one tray of cookies is golden on one side and charred on the other.
2. **The set.** Pull wide until we see the kitchen is a built set. The memory kitchen is perfect, but past its edges are raw 2x4 bracing, sandbags, a painted backdrop for the window and darkness. The facade is literally self made. This answers Josh's asks for a wide shot and exposed 2x4s, and it raises questions instead of answering them. **Claude's recommendation, ideally combined with 1.**
3. **The veneer peeling.** A glossy skin over the room curls back at one edge like contact paper or a photo decal, and the worn real room shows underneath.
4. **The double exposure.** A disposable camera double exposure, the 1995 snapshot of the memory ghosted over the real room. The flaw is era true.
5. **The shrink wrap.** The memory is preserved under plastic. Clear covers on the stools, the fruit bowl sealed, the cookie jar wrapped, like a museum period room. The "plastique fiction" made literal.
6. **The stalled renovation.** Half the orange tile has been painted white and the brush is still on the can. A ladder, a drop cloth. The new veneer stopped halfway. Also a direct line to "no money to renovate."

## Offbeat details that ask questions

Pick a few per frame. Never all of them. Each one should make the viewer ask "why is that there."

- A birthday cake on the peninsula, candles burned down to stubs, one slice gone, no plates
- The wall phone off its hook, its coiled cord pulled taut out of frame into a dark hallway
- One child's light up sneaker on the tile, still blinking
- Moving boxes from 1995 still taped shut in the corner
- Clear plastic slipcovers on the bar stools
- A fishbowl with no water and a ceramic goldfish
- Kids' drawings on the fridge faded to blank paper
- A TV on the counter glowing blue with no signal
- A water stain on the ceiling shaped like nothing in particular
- A smoke detector hanging by its wires, the battery on the counter
- Exposed studs where drywall was cut away, as if someone went looking behind the wall
- A cookie jar that is slightly too big, the memory inflating an inch

## Mood and photographic language

- **Uncanny, tender, quiet dread.** Melancholy, not menace. The room should feel like it is holding its breath.
- **Two candidate looks for the camera.** A) A staged large format tableau, very still and precise, everything placed on purpose. B) A 1995 family snapshot, point and shoot, Kodak Gold warmth, on-camera flash, slight vignette. A can photograph the memory as a set. B is how the memory was recorded. Test both.
- **Camera at a 10 year old's eye height,** about four feet off the floor, looking slightly up at the counter. The memory belongs to the kid.
- **Square 1:1,** composed as an album cover, legible as a thumbnail. One clear focal point, the cookie tray or the peninsula, with the rest rewarding a closer look.
- Spirit references (never copy, never recreate a specific work): staged suburban photography of the 1990s and 2000s, family album photography of the 80s and 90s, 70s documentary photography of the American suburbs, "liminal space" interiors, the suburban surface hiding rot in late 80s American film, and manufactured perfect towns in late 90s film.

## What three rounds taught us · keep and kill

Rounds 1 to 3 ran Nov 11 2026 in Figma on the Album Cover page (boards 411:829, 412:829, 413:829).

| Keep | Kill |
|---|---|
| 70s orange tile, no wallpaper | Any explosion, fight, fire or action, which tells a story with an answer |
| The peninsula kitchen: teak cabinets, avocado fridge and range, wood topped bar, chrome stools in orange vinyl (Josh's pick from round 2) | Cinematic crime film lighting, smoke, a war zone mood |
| A wide shot | Cookies everywhere as clutter, flour blasts, fire extinguishers |
| Exposed 2x4 studs and wiring, the truth behind the wall | The pristine brand new 1970s "before." The memory is a glowing 1995, not a showroom |
| Cracks as metaphor: a spiderweb in the tile, a split in the bar top | Piles or heaps of dough, anything mid motion |
| Liquids as quiet stains, not splashes | Violence as spectacle. The turmoil is emotional |
| The before and after idea, now folded into one frame | Two separate images. The phasing happens inside one picture |

**The core miss:** we built an event when the idea is a state of mind. Every round answered "what happened here" with a fight or a blast. The brief asks for a room where nothing happened that anyone will admit to.

## The memory kitchen · locks from Josh, Nov 12 2026

Memory round 1 (board 424:14) set these. They hold for every memory and truth render from here on. Round 2 (board 426:23) is the first round built on them.

- **Memory first.** Clean and pristine. Lock the exact kitchen before any truth version. Truth versions are edits of the kitchen Josh picks.
- **Camera.** The position of round 1 frame 9: high in a corner just under the ceiling, looking down across the room. 14mm.
- **Size.** As big as round 1 frame 9 feels. Round 1 frame 2 was close but too small, frame 8 too closed in.
- **Layout.** Never an island or a peninsula. Never a U or a galley. A real, compact working layout, not pieces spread far apart like round 1 frame 7. Round 1 frames 2 and 4 influence it.
- **Range.** Always a standard range, oven with the stovetop on top, set between two base cabinets. Never open on one side, never a wall oven or a separate cooktop.
- **Realism.** Keep it believable even on cheap explore runs: one dishwasher, one sink, one range, one refrigerator.
- **Appliances.** Green (avocado).
- **Another room.** Always a glimpse into another room, like round 1 frames 2 and 6. A glimpse of the dining table, as in frame 4, is loved.
- **TV VCR.** The TV VCR combo from the Determinator track cover sits on the kitchen counter in every render.
- **Light.** Round 1 frame 4: early dinner time after school, subtle shadows across the cabinetry.
- **No food.** No cookies, baked goods or food of any kind in any kitchen, for now.
- **Aesthetic.** Round 1 frame 4 is the best interior design overall. Frames 2 and 4 influence the next rounds.
- **Cabinets.** Favorite: round 1 frame 2 (honey oak, cathedral arch). Second: frame 6 (butter yellow slab). Never the cottage style of frames 3 and 7 (knotty pine, colonial, iron strap hinges).
- **Floor.** Round 1 frame 2 is the favorite so far, but not the final one.
- **Never.** Bright orange on white like round 1 frame 9, which does not feel common to the period.
- **Trash.** Round 1 frames 1, 3, 5, 7 and 8.

## Mandatories

- Square 1:1, about 1024 px for explore frames. Masters are made later at 4K
- No people and no hands. Traces of people only
- No readable text, no logos, no brand names. Bills, boxes and packaging stay illegible
- 70s orange tile, no wallpaper
- No piles, heaps or blobs of dough. No action frozen mid motion
- If raw dough ever appears, it is pale oatmeal beige, never pink, never wet or stringy, and never looks alive. Cookies are allowed as a single meaningful tray, not as clutter
- Never recreate an existing photograph, film still or artwork
- Explore cheaply: three frames per direction on the fast model, only the rounds Josh asks for

## Open questions for Josh

1. One image that phases between memory and truth, or a pair (memory on the front, truth on the back, a Spotify Canvas loop that dims from one to the other)?
2. Should the date stamp from a 1995 point and shoot sit in the corner? It's iconic but counts as readable text.
3. Is there a subtle place for the band's world (a chef hat on a hook, a single tray as "the first batch") or does the kitchen stand on its own?
4. Glow or snapshot: staged tableau or family photo?
5. Does this replace the dough record, or sit beside it as the back cover, a single or the zine?

## Josh's freewrite · verbatim, Nov 11 2026

> Dystopian American dream
>
> Juxtaposition of longing for nostalgia but misremembering it's emotion turmoil
>
> A family moved into a lower middle class american suburban home in 1995. The kitchen is leftover from the 70s bc theres no money to renovate. Today in 2026 we associate nostalgia to happiness. Locking away any pain or hard times from then and just choosing to keep the best parts, or even coating those parts further with a blanket of plastique fiction, painted with faux additions that were never there to begin with, but now feel real in the memory.
>
> Nostalgia is so strong that it can even provoke a deep sadness for longing. A minds eye picture manifested overtime, day by day for decades, each mental revisit subconsciously promoting a new minor false reality further up the pedestal.
>
> A self-made facade that inflates just an inch every time we visit it.
>
> Yesterday had great times, good times, hard times, traumatic times.
>
> Today has great times, good times, hard times, traumatic times.
>
> Why does nostalgia deny the ugly of yesterday? A coping mechanism used today when ugly rears it's head in real time. "It was all so much easier back then." Comforting words to hide from today's truth by time traveling to a lie.
>
> This conundrum brought to life in an image. Focused to a snapshot of the epitome of youth during the 1990's rendition of the American dream. Neither bountifully rich, nor desolate and poor. The common denominator experience now perverted inside those grown adult's minds today. Nostalgia now breaking. Phasing between white picket fakes and long-buried true struggles. The protective emotional hologram beginning to dim.
>
> The American dream then vs the memory of it today.
>
> The American dream today vs the pining for it then.
>
> Yesterday anthropomorphized with it's real cracks brought to the surface. Overcorrected into an emotional composition of disarray and turmoil. Bizarre offbeat elements scattered throughout the unusual scene which yield more curious questions than answers as to what happened. That perfect memory stolen and washed of its veneer, leaving behind a mystifying rendition we refuse to concede to and recognize as reality. Instead furthering the denial by convincing ourselves this must be the perversion. Our warm middle class kitchen staged to confuse us with an existence which never happened. Bring back our vision of nostaliga. Perfectly perfect. No cracks in sight. Every bobble back exactly where we manufactured it. Refusal to look for what we might remember.

# Creative Brief · The Kitchen · Wish Book and Flash

Album cover concept for **The First Batch** by **Chillsbury Doughboys** (CBDB). v2, written Nov 11 2026 with Josh Titus (creative director), replacing the v1 brief from the same day. The idea started with Aaron at band practice Nov 11 2026, Jon joined in, and Josh grew it into a wrapper around the nine catalog singles. Josh's freewrite is in `kitchen-brief-freewrite.md`. Hand this whole file to a new session.

## The idea

**We don't remember the 90s. We remember the catalog.**

Ask anyone who grew up in middle America what their 1995 kitchen looked like and they describe something closer to a magazine catalog page than the room they stood in. Memory fills its gaps with the pictures we stared at most, and for a 90s kid that was the wish book they circled in marker. Nostalgia isn't a record of childhood. It's an ad for it we mistook for a memory.

The nine track singles are already catalog spreads from a 1990s magazine, scanned today. The album is the house that magazine got mailed to, seen two ways.

- **Wish Book · front cover · pristine, calm.** The kitchen as memory renders it, which is to say as a 1995 magazine catalog page. Scanned today.
- **Flash · back cover · destroyed, anxious.** The same kitchen as the era actually recorded itself, a disposable camera flash snapshot at night, printed at the drugstore as a 4x6. Scanned today.

The whole package becomes one drawer of 1995 paper found and scanned in 2026. We only meet the past as artifacts.

Josh's freewrite ends with us refusing the truth by calling it the perversion. So the memory sits on the front and the back is built to feel like the fake. A real 1995 flash snapshot already looks almost too harsh to believe.

## Why this mechanism

- **It welds the cover to the singles.** Wish Book uses the singles' photographic recipe (medium format, soft glow behind every object, muted print color), so the viewer recognizes the memory as product photography before they can say why.
- **The phasing is photographic language itself.** Every 90s kid reads catalog photography and flash snapshots fluently. Nothing needs decoding, which keeps it a common American experience instead of an art school metaphor.
- **No tropes.** No neon, no VHS glitch, no cassette and pencil, no mall. The unease comes from realizing your warmest memory is lit like a sales page.
- **Every bobble back exactly where we manufactured it.** Both versions share one camera and one room, and every object in one has a counterpart in the same spot in the other. Front and back become spot the difference.

## The room · shared by both versions

- **When and who.** 1995. A lower middle class family of four in a suburban starter home they moved into that year. No people, only their traces.
- **The kitchen.** Never renovated since the 1970s because there was never money. Glossy burnt orange tile backsplash, no wallpaper anywhere. Teak slab cabinets. Avocado refrigerator on the left wall, avocado range on the back wall. A window over the sink on the back wall. A doorway to a hallway on the right wall, a beige wall phone and a key hook beside it. A wood topped breakfast bar peninsula across the foreground with two chrome bar stools in orange vinyl. Sheet linoleum floor. Flat white ceiling with a smoke detector.
- **Camera.** About four feet off the floor, a ten year old's eye height. Straight on, level, wide lens, square 1:1. The peninsula top sits at the eye line, so the cookie tray is the focal point.

## The object map · memory, then truth

Nine positions. Each line is the same spot in both frames.

1. **Window over the sink.** Wish Book · a sunny back yard with a white picket fence, flat and slightly too perfect, like a painted backdrop. Flash · black glass holding only the hot white reflection of the camera's own flash.
2. **Counter and sill.** Wish Book · the 1985 dark brown wedge clock radio from Boombap on the sill, its red clock reading 6:30. The charcoal 19 inch TV VCR combo from Determinator on the counter right of the sink, screen dark and reflecting the sunny window. Flash · the clock radio is gone and a nearly empty glass coin jar stands in its place. The TV glows no signal blue and the VCR clock blinks 12:00, the sign the power was cut and came back.
3. **Refrigerator.** Wish Book · the silver dual deck boombox from Deluge on top. Magnets in all nine track colors holding crisp kids' crayon drawings. Flash · on top, a bread machine still sealed in its box, the wedding gift nobody used. The drawings faded to nearly blank paper, a pink notice under one magnet, one magnet fallen to the floor.
4. **Hallway door.** Wish Book · the beige wall phone on its hook, cord neatly coiled. The translucent pink virtual pet from Suavecita hanging on the key hook. Flash · the receiver off the hook, its coiled cord pulled taut out of frame down the dark hallway. The virtual pet's screen blank. On the hallway floor, the silver 1993 Talkboy from Show Me Your Love, side profile, aimed into the kitchen, record light on.
5. **Peninsula left · the catalog.** Wish Book · a magazine lies open to a catalog spread of nine product photos on colored grounds in a 3 by 3 grid, wheel order wrapped clockwise from the top left (Blueberry, Butter, Cherry, Bondi, Bubblegum, Lime, Grape, Tangerine), Mint in the middle. Nothing marked. Flash · the same spread, dog eared, a coffee ring, marker circles drawn around the things that never came.
6. **Peninsula center · the tray.** Wish Book · the first batch, golden cookies cooling in rows on a wire rack. Flash · the same tray holding raw dough balls in neat rows, never baked. Behind it the oven door hangs open and the oven is dark and cold. The title pays off here. Memory baked it.
7. **Peninsula right.** Wish Book · a bowl of glossy plastic fruit. Flash · bills in pink and yellow fanned out, a pocket calculator, a mug of cold coffee.
8. **Stools.** Wish Book · the yellow sports Walkman from Spice on the left seat, headphones beside it. Flash · orange vinyl split to the foam, both seats empty.
9. **Ceiling.** Wish Book · a warm patch of sun. Flash · a brown water stain bloomed wide and sagging, the smoke detector hanging by its wires, its battery on the counter.

**Everywhere else in Flash.** Lifted linoleum edges, a spiderweb crack through the orange tile, grout gone grey, a square of drywall cut out beside the refrigerator showing studs and wiring, one cabinet door hanging off a hinge, an orange extension cord across the floor. One child's light up sneaker on the tile.

**Everywhere else in Wish Book.** No electrical cord, plug or outlet anywhere in the frame. Catalogs hide cords, and this is the quiet tell that the memory is an ad. Every surface waxed, glossy, shrink wrap clean.

Six of the nine devices appear. Bopkins (turntable), Traycee (PC) and Mancub (handheld console) stay out because they don't live in a kitchen. The nine colors carry through both frames in the plastics, the magnets and the catalog spread. In Flash they sit under the green fluorescent cast but stay recognizable.

## The two photographic languages

**Wish Book.** A full page photograph from a 1995 home catalog, shot on medium format film, printed in a magazine and scanned today. Warm late morning sun from the window. Soft glow behind every object and on the floor like the singles, falling a shade deeper in the top corners. Soft contact shadows only, no visible light source. Muted print color, fine grain, crisp true detail. Everything still.

**Flash.** A 4x6 drugstore print from a 1995 disposable camera, shot at about 11 PM with the on camera flash, scanned today. Warm Kodak Gold color, the flash hot on the nearest surfaces, the room falling off fast into dark corners, slight vignette, hard flash shadows behind objects. The only other light is one fluorescent tube under the cabinet over the sink, a faint green cast. Everything still. "Destroyed" means decay and strain, never an event. No smoke, fire, falling water, debris in the air or anything mid motion.

Neither version shows paper edges, a print border, a page gutter or a fold. Full bleed, one surface. The scan reads through color, grain and print character only, matching the singles where halftone and paper textures are off for now.

## How it ships

- **Front cover** · Wish Book. At 64 px it reads as a warm square, a bright window, a golden tray and a tiny nine color grid.
- **Back cover** · Flash. At 64 px it reads as a dark square, a white flash spot, a blue screen and a green bar of light. Track list type is set over it later in Figma.
- **Spotify Canvas** · Wish Book with the under cabinet tube flickering. Each flicker cuts to Flash for a frame or two, a little more often each pass, then the loop resets. The hologram dimming, and the only place on Spotify the back cover gets seen. Gravity rules still hold, nothing in the room moves.
- **Vinyl gatefold, if vinyl goes** · the inside spread can pull the same kitchen wide as a lit set braced by raw 2x4s, the v1 set idea kept as decoration on the concept.

## Readable text · Claude's call Nov 11 2026, Josh to veto

Two exceptions, both digits on device clocks, following the tuner scale precedent on Spice. **6:30** on the clock radio in Wish Book, the same time the Boombap single shows. **12:00** blinking on the VCR in Flash. The memory stopped at breakfast, the truth reset in the dark. Everything else stays illegible: the catalog, bills, notice, bread machine box, drawings. The Talkboy carries no script in this image.

The Talkboy recording from the hallway is the heaviest note in the set. It stays in until Josh says otherwise, and it can come out without changing anything else.

## Production · plate, then branch

Explore follows CLAUDE.md. Nano Banana 2 Flash, three frames per direction, Review boards in Figma on the Photo Pipeline page, Josh picks by number.

1. **Plate.** Generate the empty room with the plate prompt below. Three frames. Josh picks one. It fixes the camera and the geometry for everything after.
2. **Branch.** From the picked plate, run the Wish Book edit and the Flash edit as separate "keep this exact photograph" edits, three frames each. Branching both from the same parent holds the geometry far better than editing Flash out of Wish Book, since a day to night flash relight is where Gemini edits drift.
3. **Pair check.** Josh picks one of each. Overlay them at 50 percent and confirm the window, peninsula edge, fridge and doorway line up. Canvas depends on it. If they drift, re-run the weaker branch from the plate.
4. **4K.** Only after Josh's fresh written yes. Render each pick at 1:1 4K as its own edit. Run the overlay check again on the 4K pair.
5. **Canvas.** Canvas is 9:16 and the covers are square. A center crop keeps the window, the counter and the tray but loses the fridge and the hallway. Either accept that crop or extend both 4K frames vertically with the same edit. Decide with Josh after step 3.

## What could go wrong

- **Wish Book reads as a nice retro kitchen and nothing more.** The cord rule, the painted yard and the catalog glow are the safeguards. If frames still read stock, push the glow harder before adding props.
- **Flash tips into misery bait.** No people and no event keep it at quiet dread. The Talkboy is the one element to pull if it does.
- **Text creep.** Bills, the box and the catalog want to grow words. Prompts name them illegible, and the check step reads every frame for it.
- **Prop overload.** The map is dense on purpose for the back cover game, but the front must hold one focal point. If Wish Book clutters, drop the boombox and the Walkman first.
- **Generated devices drift from the singles.** The kitchen devices only need to rhyme with the singles at small size. They are props here, not portraits.
- **Raw dough reads alive.** Dough balls in even rows, no pairs that read as eyes, never pink, never wet.

## Mandatories

- Square 1:1, about 1024 px for explore frames, 4K for masters
- No people and no hands, traces only
- No logos, no brand names, no readable text beyond 6:30 and 12:00
- 70s orange tile, no wallpaper
- No piles, heaps or blobs of dough, nothing mid motion
- Raw dough is pale oatmeal beige, firm, never pink, wet or stringy, never alive
- Never recreate an existing photograph, film still or artwork
- Explore cheaply, three frames per direction on Flash, only the rounds Josh asks for

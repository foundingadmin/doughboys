# Do before release day, not after

One part of this cannot wait until the album is live.

- **Pitch at least 7 days before release.** Spotify for Artists lets us pitch one unreleased song to the playlist editors. A pitch in by that deadline also drops the song into every follower's Release Radar. Miss the window and both are gone for that release
- **One song per pitch.** The next pitch opens once the pitched song is live. So the singles plan (still open in the brief) decides how many pitches the album gets
- **The pitch form rewards detail.** Genre, mood, culture tags, the story behind the song, our marketing plan. The art direction doc already holds most of the story and the visuals

# Canvas · the looping video behind each track

Studio at [canvas.spotify.com](https://canvas.spotify.com). Josh's source video [here](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kL62WKmrZOs&t=6s).

- Visual identity on the Now Playing screen, the one place a listener stares at our art while the song plays
- Can be removed, replaced or changed anytime, as often as we want. Low risk to test and swap
- **Specs** · vertical 9:16, 3 to 8 seconds, 720 to 1080 px tall, MP4 or JPG
- **Spotify's guidance** · no talking or singing on camera, no rapid cuts or heavy flashing, keep key visuals off the edges (some phones crop), skip the song and band name since the player already shows them
- **Loop types Josh noted**
  1. Continuous loop · the last frame flows back into the first
  2. Hard cut repeat · plays through, cuts back to the start
  3. Rebound · plays forward, then in reverse

**Conflict to settle.** The brief locks "Canvas loops are gravity only and never play in reverse" (dough is lifeless). Rebound breaks that, since reverse makes dough rise. Continuous or hard cut fits the rule. Josh's call if he wants to reopen it.

**Already in place.** Every track already has a 9:16 Canvas board in Figma (Album Art page), exported at 1080 × 1920, which fits the spec. Those are stills. Motion is the next step.

# How listeners find new music

Josh's source video [here](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sDC4jcv5O-8).

1. **User playlists** · made by listeners themselves
2. **Editorial playlists** · a team of editors runs Spotify's mood, vibe and genre lists. A song that listeners like climbs into other lists. The pitch above is the way in
3. **Algorithmic playlists** · unique to each listener. Daily Mixes, Release Radar and similar, built from predictions of what one person will play next. Possible because of dense metadata attached to each song, crowdsourced automatically from listening behavior

What feeds the algorithm on our side is early listener behavior (saves, follows, adds to playlists, finishing the song) plus accurate genre and mood tags at pitch time.

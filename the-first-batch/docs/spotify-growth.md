# Step zero · we have no Spotify presence yet

As of Nov 9 2026 the band has no Spotify for Artists account and nothing on Spotify. Everything below depends on these, in order.

1. **Distributor · Wiley owns this.** As of Nov 9 2026 Wiley is comparing CD Baby and DistroKid, and we will use one. Not Josh's lane, so this doc assumes it gets done. Spotify takes music only through a distributor or label, and the distributor creates our artist profile when it delivers the first release. One thing worth passing to Wiley is that a Spotify preferred provider makes the step 3 access request instant
2. **Upload the album with a release date set weeks out.** That gives time for step 3's review (a few days if manual) and the 7 day pitch window. The distributor then hands us the artist link and the release's UPC (Universal Product Code)
3. **Claim Spotify for Artists before release.** Request access at [artists.spotify.com](https://artists.spotify.com). Without a preferred distributor, Spotify reviews by hand and wants a website or social link to prove we are the band, so the Instagram account should exist first. For a profile with no live music yet, send Spotify support the artist link plus the upcoming release's UPC. Only one request can be pending per profile, so one person files it and invites the rest of the band as team members
4. **Then pitch, then Canvas.** Pitch one song 7+ days ahead, upload Canvas loops once the tracks show in the dashboard

# Do before release day, not after

One part of this cannot wait until the album is live.

- **Pitch at least 7 days before release.** Spotify for Artists lets us pitch one unreleased song to the playlist editors. A pitch in by that deadline also drops the song into every follower's Release Radar. Miss the window and both are gone for that release
- **One song per pitch.** The next pitch opens once the pitched song is live. So the singles plan (still open in the brief) decides how many pitches the album gets
- **The pitch form rewards detail.** Genre, mood, culture tags, the story behind the song, our marketing plan. The art direction doc already holds most of the story and the visuals

# Canvas · the looping video behind each track

Studio at [canvas.spotify.com](https://canvas.spotify.com). Josh's source video [here](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kL62WKmrZOs&t=6s).

- Visual identity on the Now Playing screen, the one place a listener stares at our art while the song plays
- Can be removed, replaced or changed anytime, as often as we want. Low risk to test and swap
- **Specs** · vertical 9:16, 3 to 8 seconds, 720 to 1080 px tall, MP4 or JPG
- **Spotify's guidance** · no talking or singing on camera, no rapid cuts or heavy flashing, keep key visuals off the edges (some phones crop), skip the song and band name since the player already shows them
- **Loop types Josh noted**
  1. Continuous loop · the last frame flows back into the first
  2. Hard cut repeat · plays through, cuts back to the start
  3. Rebound · plays forward, then in reverse

**Conflict to settle.** The brief locks "Canvas loops are gravity only and never play in reverse" (dough is lifeless). Rebound breaks that, since reverse makes dough rise. Continuous or hard cut fits the rule. Josh's call if he wants to reopen it.

**Already in place.** Every track already has a 9:16 Canvas board in Figma (Album Art page), exported at 1080 × 1920, which fits the spec. Those are stills. Motion is the next step.

# How listeners find new music

Josh's source video [here](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sDC4jcv5O-8).

1. **User playlists** · made by listeners themselves
2. **Editorial playlists** · a team of editors runs Spotify's mood, vibe and genre lists. A song that listeners like climbs into other lists. The pitch above is the way in
3. **Algorithmic playlists** · unique to each listener. Daily Mixes, Release Radar and similar, built from predictions of what one person will play next. Possible because of dense metadata attached to each song, crowdsourced automatically from listening behavior

What feeds the algorithm on our side is early listener behavior (saves, follows, adds to playlists, finishing the song) plus accurate genre and mood tags at pitch time.

# Don't buy playlist campaigns

From a Reddit post Josh saved Nov 9 2026 (r/musicmarketing, a former label marketer). Secondhand and unverified, but it lines up with how the algorithm section above works.

- **How the "organic playlist" pitch really works.** Agencies pay playlist owners about $10 to $15 per 1,000 streams, and those owners fill their playlists with paid ads. The streams and listeners are real, but nobody chose us. They pressed play on "Deep Focus" and stayed for the playlist
- **The pattern.** A big spike during the campaign, the song drops off the playlist, daily streams fall back to where they started, and almost no new followers or saves
- **What Spotify actually weighs.** Saves, repeat listens, profile visits, people finishing the song, and people looking us up on purpose. Raw stream count matters far less
- **The active listener ratio.** The post says Spotify wants more than half of all listeners to be active (listeners who chose us) and treats that as more important than total listeners. It's folklore with no source, so treat it as directional only

**What this means for us.** Every listener we bring in should be someone who came looking for the band. That's a job for assets we're already making. The stickers, the Bread Clip pin backing card, the zine and the Instagram posts can each send people straight to our Spotify profile with one ask, save the album. A small crowd that saves and replays beats a big passive one.

A real 1990 mail order catalog photograph shot on medium format film with an 80mm lens, fine film grain, muted print color. Shot straight down from directly overhead, the camera square to the surface, a flat top down view in a square frame.

Nine standard audio cassette tapes lie in a shingled stack running down the frame, each one overlapping the one below so only the top edge strip of each shows, like a fanned deck of cards. Together they read as nine horizontal color bands filling the frame edge to edge. Each cassette shell is translucent tinted plastic in one muted color, top to bottom in this exact order: blueberry blue, butter yellow, cherry red, bondi blue, bubblegum pink, lime green, grape purple, tangerine orange, mint green. The bottom cassette, in mint green, shows whole: instead of tape, its two reels and its window are packed with raw bread dough that bulges softly out of the window. Real size cassettes, about 10 by 6.5 centimeters.

Firm and moist, freshly mixed raw bread dough, thick, heavy, plump and saggy, one cohesive mass in one even pale oatmeal beige like raw pizza dough, never pink and never meaty, never tinted by the colors around it. Satin matte skin with fine stretch lines and soft folds. Never dry or stringy: no strands, threads or torn edges, no holes, no lace, no webbing. Nothing runny, wet, slimy or glossy. Shaped only by gravity, an object and never a creature.

Soft diffused light from the upper left, from a source well outside the frame, soft contact shadows between the shells.

No people, no hands, no logos, no brand names, no readable text. No accidental faces: no pair of dimples, bubbles or holes that read as eyes. No label words on the cassettes.

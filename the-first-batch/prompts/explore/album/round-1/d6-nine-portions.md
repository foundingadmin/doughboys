A real 1990 mail order catalog photograph shot on medium format film with an 80mm lens, fine film grain, muted print color. Shot straight down from directly overhead, the camera square to the surface, a flat top down view in a square frame.

A worn aluminum baking sheet fills the frame edge to edge, its rolled rim just outside the frame. On it, nine squares of matte parchment paper in a 3 by 3 grid with thin gaps of bare metal between them, each square tinted one solid muted color.

Top row, left to right: blueberry blue, butter yellow, cherry red.
Middle row, left to right: tangerine orange, mint green, bondi blue.
Bottom row, left to right: grape purple, lime green, bubblegum pink.

On each square sits one hand portioned lump of raw bread dough, all about the same size, each one a different irregular shape: one slumped low and wide, one folded over on itself, one pinched into a soft ridge, one with a deep dent where it was pressed, one leaning, one long and lazy. Never a perfect round ball, never symmetrical, never coiled or stacked.

Firm and moist, freshly mixed raw bread dough, thick, heavy, plump and saggy, one cohesive mass in one even pale oatmeal beige like raw pizza dough, never pink and never meaty. Satin matte skin with fine stretch lines and soft folds. Never dry or stringy: no strands, threads or torn edges stretching between pieces, no holes, no lace, no webbing. No pool, no puddle, nothing runny, wet, slimy or glossy. Shaped only by gravity, an object and never a creature.

Soft diffused light from the upper left, from a source well outside the frame, soft contact shadow under each lump.

No people, no hands, no logos, no brand names, no readable text. No accidental faces: no pair of dimples, bubbles or holes that read as eyes.

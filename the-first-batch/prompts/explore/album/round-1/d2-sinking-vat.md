A real 1990 mail order catalog photograph shot on medium format film with an 80mm lens, fine film grain, muted print color. Shot straight down from directly overhead, the camera square to the surface, a flat top down view in a square frame.

Looking straight down into a huge square vat of raw bread dough, so wide that the dough fills the frame edge to edge and no rim shows. The frame covers about 80 centimeters of dough.

Firm and moist, freshly mixed raw bread dough, thick, heavy, plump and saggy, one cohesive mass in one even pale oatmeal beige like raw pizza dough, never pink and never meaty. Satin matte skin with fine stretch lines and soft folds. Never dry or stringy: no strands, threads or torn edges stretching between pieces, no holes, no lace, no webbing. No pool, no puddle, nothing runny, wet, slimy or glossy. Shaped only by gravity, an object and never a creature.

Three real 1990 electronics have sunk deep into the dough. Only small pieces still poke out of the surface, the dough closing snugly around each one with a soft rounded collar where it meets the plastic or metal:
1. The top corner of a classic grey handheld game console, only its last 6 centimeters, the light grey plastic, the rounded corner and the edge of the darker grey screen frame, tipped at an angle. It looks like image 1.
2. The chrome telescoping antenna of a silver dual deck boombox, only the thin rod, pencil thin and about 25 centimeters long, sticking out of the dough at a low slant with its small ball tip. It looks like image 2.
3. One corner of a dark brown 1985 bedside digital clock radio, its wood grain slatted top and the edge of its wedge shaped cabinet, about 12 centimeters of it showing, tilted. It looks like image 3.

Every piece is at true real world size, relative to each other and to the dough: the antenna pencil thin, the console corner smaller than a palm, the clock radio corner the biggest piece. They sit far apart across the frame with wide calm stretches of dough between them, off center, like things lost in the vat. Gravity only, the dough is lifeless.

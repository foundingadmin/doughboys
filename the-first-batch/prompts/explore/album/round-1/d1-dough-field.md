A real 1990 mail order catalog photograph shot on medium format film with an 80mm lens, fine film grain, muted print color. Shot straight down from directly overhead, the camera square to the surface, a flat top down view in a square frame.

The whole frame is raw bread dough, edge to edge, and nothing else: the surface of a huge slab of dough so wide that no rim, bowl, table or background shows anywhere. The frame covers about 60 centimeters of dough.

Firm and moist, freshly mixed raw bread dough, thick, heavy, plump and saggy, one cohesive mass in one even pale oatmeal beige like raw pizza dough, never pink and never meaty. Satin matte skin with fine stretch lines and soft folds. Never dry or stringy: no strands, threads or torn edges stretching between pieces, no holes, no lace, no webbing. No pool, no puddle, nothing runny, wet, slimy or glossy. Shaped only by gravity, an object and never a creature.

Subtle texture across the whole surface: fine stretch lines, a few soft shallow creases and lazy folds where the dough settled under its own weight, tiny bubbles just under the satin skin, a faint dusting of flour in one area. Mostly calm and even. The creases are quiet, irregular and few, never a pattern, never a swirl. Low soft raking light from the upper left, from a source well outside the frame, so the creases read as gentle shadow and highlight.

No people, no hands, no logos, no brand names, no readable text. No accidental faces: no pair of dimples, bubbles or holes that read as eyes.

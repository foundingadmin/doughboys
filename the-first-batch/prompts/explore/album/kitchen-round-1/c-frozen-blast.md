A real 1970s American home magazine photograph shot on medium format film with a high speed strobe that freezes motion, fine film grain, muted warm print color like a 1974 Kodachrome page.

The exact instant of a flour explosion in a 1970s American home kitchen, frozen in midair. A harvest gold stand mixer on an avocado green countertop with chrome trim is the center of a huge billowing white cloud of flour bursting outward in every direction. Caught in the air inside the blast: walnut laminate cabinet doors flung off their hinges, wooden spoons, a whisk, eggshells, a measuring cup, a rolling pin, sugar crystals, a few glass mixing bowls in blueberry blue, cherry red and tangerine, and chunks of raw bread dough flying as rounded lumps. Behind it, mustard and brown floral wallpaper and a window with a café curtain blown sideways. Flour already coating the counter and the orange and brown geometric linoleum floor.

Raw dough: freshly mixed raw bread dough in one even pale oatmeal beige like raw pizza dough, never pink and never meaty, satin matte, thick plump rounded lumps. No strands, threads, lace or webbing. Inanimate, never a creature, no accidental faces.

Camera straight on at countertop height, squared up to the mixer, the blast centered and filling most of the square frame, composed like an album cover, with the whole cloud inside the frame.

No people, no hands, no logos, no brand names, no readable text.
